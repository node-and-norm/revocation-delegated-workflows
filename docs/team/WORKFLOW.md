# Research assignment workflow

Version 0.2 · 3 November 2026 · Draft for Mark Julius Banasihan's review

A research assignment should leave another reviewer able to trace the question, evidence, method, and decision.  This workflow organizes that record and supports the [team charter](CHARTER.md).  The research lead approves scope and consequential next steps.  Each contributor records the work actually performed.

## 1. Define the question and assignment

State the unresolved problem, the intended user of the result, and the decision the work will inform.  Name the study, owner, permitted actions, deliverable, and stopping condition.  List established facts, working assumptions, and missing evidence separately.

For the present study, preserve the completed v0.3 integration record and the execution-grounded revocation agenda.  The complementary reconstruction exercise first needs a packet specification and scoring rubric.  Its current status is design-only.

A useful assignment brief answers five questions: What must we learn?  Why does it matter?  Which evidence can answer it?  What may the contributor do?  What would justify stopping?

## 2. Establish the evidence boundary

Inventory exact input versions and their locations.  Distinguish the information available to the original decision-maker, the evidence supplied to a later reviewer, and the evaluator's retained reference record.  Differences between those information sets can determine which conclusions are justified.

For every source, capture its author or provider, locator, date, status, relevant claim, limitations, and reuse conditions.  Cite the section supporting a consequential statement.  Record access failures explicitly.  Identify duplicated reports and common-source dependence before counting corroboration.

For a cross-study connection, add the proposed mechanism and the closest competing explanation.  A catalog association can guide the search for evidence.  The evidence review determines whether the proposed relationship survives scrutiny.

## 3. Develop the research contributions

The evidence researcher maps prior work and alternative explanations.  The methods researcher specifies the smallest useful test, a competent baseline, controls, measures, and rejection criteria.  The critical reviewer checks both contributions against primary sources and searches for decisive counterexamples.

Each handoff carries the question, input versions, action limits, expected output, known uncertainty, and relevant earlier decisions.  Contributors should label observations, interpretations, and recommendations.  Alfred integrates the contributions while preserving unresolved disagreements and their consequences for the next decision.

## 4. Assess contribution and feasibility

The team examines the proposed study before committing to execution:

- What would it add to existing methods, instruments, or evidence?
- Could ordinary retrieval and a competent checklist answer the question?
- Which observation would reject the claim or make the exercise uninformative?
- Can the evidence distinguish the competing explanations?
- Are data rights, privacy protections, effort, and execution permissions adequate?

Record a decision to continue, revise, replicate explicitly, or stop.  State the reason and the supporting evidence.  A well-supported negative finding or decision to stop can prevent unnecessary work.  Originality claims require a bounded account of the literature searched and the distinctions established.

## 5. Fix the evaluation design

Before an authorized evaluation, save the protocol, packet-construction rules, comparison methods, scoring rubric, source versions, and planned analysis.  Keep raw records separate from derivative packets.  Document redactions and extraction steps so another reviewer can reproduce the packet from its source.

For a blinded assessment, the data custodian holds the case map and reference answers separately.  Reviewers receive the evidence specified by the protocol.  Record prior exposure, shared access, and accidental answer cues.  Case names, collection order, record lengths, and metadata can reveal answers even after obvious labels have been removed.

Score whether the supplied evidence justifies a conclusion separately from whether the answer matches hidden reference truth.  A justified abstention should receive the credit defined by the rubric.  Report case-level engineering checks with their actual unit of analysis and dependence structure.

Model proofs, source inspections, runtime tests, and human reviews each require a clear description of their scope.  Evaluation execution, participant involvement, and model/API use remain subject to the applicable approval.  A documentation update leaves the execution status unchanged.

### Jev comparison record

For any approved Jev evaluation, record the semantic question, allowed labels, evidence supplied, model and SDK versions, output-validation rules, abstention rule, and deterministic checks applied before the model call.  Keep semantic judgments separate from authorization decisions.  Compare the same cases under equal evidence access and review budgets, and preserve errors and rejected outputs without silent repair.  Report calibration, classification errors, abstention coverage, latency, and cost separately.  Earlier TAE × Jev results should be cited with their original scope.

## 6. Preserve and examine results

During authorized execution, retain inputs, code, configuration, environment information, outputs, failures, and corrections.  Give new runs distinct locations.  Preserve historical results alongside later checks so readers can see which criteria applied at each stage.

Reproduction and separate review should state exactly what was checked, what failed, and what remains unexamined.  The quality reviewer should compare the actual instrument behavior with the intended measurement.  The integrity reviewer should check source attribution, rights, AI assistance, and the account of human review.

For reconstruction work, assess reported disposition, established attempts, cumulative effects, initiator provenance, event ordering, record coverage, and unresolved obligations separately.  A denied retry may follow an earlier completed effect.  A framework's successful message handling can also carry a denial of the protected action.  The rubric should explain these distinctions through the recorded fields and their documented meanings.

## 7. Resolve review findings

Give each review finding a short identifier, evidence locator, consequence, and proposed response.  Record whether the team corrected the issue, accepted a limitation, or left the question unresolved.  A rejected concern requires supporting evidence.  Preserve material disagreement for Mark Julius Banasihan's decision.

A correction may affect more than one artifact.  Check the protocol, code, results, summary, workbook, and notebook for dependent claims.  Update the relevant current records while preserving historical versions and explaining the change.

## 8. Deliver a decision brief and handoff

The final brief should state the finding, supporting evidence, strongest alternative explanation, limits, unresolved disagreement, and recommended next decision.  Link each substantive claim to its canonical artifact.  Describe material AI assistance and verified human contributions accurately.

A complete assignment record includes:

- Assignment and study identifiers, owner, and date.
- Question, scope, authority, and stopping condition.
- Input versions, source locators, and access or reuse restrictions.
- Contributors by role and recorded tools or models.
- Claims, counterclaims, and supporting evidence.
- Checks performed, failures, corrections, and unresolved issues.
- Decision, authorized next step, artifact locations, and review status.

These records provide continuity across sessions and studies.  A future automated runtime would need a separate review of permissions, resource limits, logging, stop behavior, and tests.

## Maintain the long-term decision record

Give each material future decision an identifier and retain its status: proposed, under review, approved within a defined scope, deferred, declined, superseded, or completed.  Record the date, decision owner, options considered, evidence, rationale, constraints, unresolved questions, and revisit trigger.  Link later decisions to the entries they supersede so readers can follow the change in reasoning.

Capture decisions about specialist roles, shared data, environments, human review, multi-study coordination, and automation.  Keep the approved scope beside the chosen option.  A future possibility should remain visibly proposed until the decision and applicable authorization are recorded.

Before closing an assignment, reconcile its decision record with the brief, review findings, and research lead's directions.  Identify known gaps in capture.  This check supports a faithful account of what the team considered and avoids implying that every discussion was recorded exhaustively.

## Writing and editorial review

Use the E5 principles recovered from the research lead's writing materials: establish the problem early, name the actor, place the action in a clear verb, keep modifiers close to their targets, and make pronoun references precise.  Explain the mechanism connecting evidence to a claim.  Place qualifications beside the claim they limit.

Expand a section when the reader needs a missing reason, definition, example, or procedure.  Remove repeated cautions and introductory filler.  Use direct statements in place of formulaic contrast patterns.  Check parallel structure, sentence boundaries, attribution, and the strength of causal language.  The final prose should help a first-time reader understand both the work and the decision it supports.
