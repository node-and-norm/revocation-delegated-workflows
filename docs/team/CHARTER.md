# AI-assisted research team charter

Version 0.1 · 3 November 2026 · Draft for Mark Banasihan's review

## Purpose and present status

Help Node & Norm produce bounded, reproducible research on whether institutional authority is respected in delegated AI workflows and what evidence supports that judgment. Frontier-AI assurance and AGI readiness are longer-term purposes, not claims established by this pilot.

The team is a set of repeatable research roles, not a continuously running autonomous organization. This charter records a proposed working arrangement. It creates no runtime, credentials, schedule, budget, human appointments or additional permission. AI sessions may change; reviewed artifacts, sources, decisions and handoff records provide continuity.

## People and AI roles

| Role | Responsibility | Required output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mark, human research lead | Chooses questions, scope, consequential claims and release decisions; retains decision authority | Recorded decisions and unresolved questions |
| Alfred, AI research coordinator | Defines bounded assignments, assembles sources and findings, resolves inconsistencies where evidence permits, and presents decisions | One coherent brief with disagreement and limits visible |
| Literature and evidence researcher, AI-assisted | Reads primary sources, maps overlap, checks source support and reuse conditions | Source-linked claim and prior-work comparison |
| Methods researcher, AI-assisted | Specifies cases, controls, measurements, alternatives and rejection criteria before evaluation | Versioned protocol and evaluation boundary |
| Critical reviewer, AI-assisted | Challenges assumptions, provenance, leakage, scoring and overstatement against original evidence | Findings, counterexamples and disposition of each concern |

A separate AI review is not independent human validation. Agreement among assistants is not corroboration when they share sources, models or assumptions. Do not attribute human expertise or employment to an AI role. A fresh review should receive the primary evidence and criteria, not just another assistant's conclusions.

## Short term: one study, one bounded next question

Keep the execution-grounded revocation question as the active study. Preserve the completed qualification and integration records and their agenda. A retrospective evidence exercise complements that work; it does not replace the enforcement question. Evidence veracity and oversight scaling remain deferred as separate study programs; a proposed evidence-reconstruction exercise is a bounded extension of the current study, not approval to activate them.

The immediate design question is what a later reviewer can establish from different portions of the existing record. Two saved framework cases report the same revoked denial, while their cumulative effect histories differ. The earlier effect in one case was inserted by the trusted controller. This is an information distinction in deliberately selected synthetic records, not a discovered production failure.

Before evaluation, prepare a packet specification, evidence-entitlement rubric, comparison against competent structured source-linked review, leakage assessment, and explicit stop criteria. Distinguish the resumed attempt, cumulative effects, actor provenance, event ordering and unknowns. Existing data do not establish a novel mechanism or reviewer performance. This charter does not approve packet evaluation, model calls, participants or new workflow runs.

## Long term: coordinated studies without premature expansion

Expand only when another bounded study has a defensible question, feasible evidence, an owner and a decision to proceed. Maintain one canonical evidence location per study; link rather than silently duplicate or rewrite prior results.

Potential specialist roles:

- **Data custodian:** source inventory, provenance, access, licenses, deduplication, versioning, immutable originals and documented derivatives.
- **Test-environment specialist:** reproducible dependencies, isolation assumptions, runtime configuration and separation of proposed, qualification and measured runs.
- **Quality reviewer:** instrument tests, leakage checks, negative controls, reproducibility and whether measured quantities match claims.
- **Research-integrity reviewer:** attribution, AI-use disclosure, rights, errors, conflicting evidence, human-review status and release limits.

These are responsibilities to allocate as needs arise, not existing appointments or scheduled agents. More roles do not automatically improve research quality. Add one when a concrete workload or conflict of responsibilities warrants it.

A shared Node & Norm methods repository may become useful when multiple active studies genuinely reuse the same charter and templates. Until then, this private repository is the home for the current study's team documents. Do not migrate other studies or create a central dataset merely because this plan mentions them.

## Connections across the research collection

The existing [Governance Explorer](https://nodeandnorm.org/governance-map) provides a discovery aid. Its reviewed website data contains eight research programs, 80 unique governance topics and 640 editorial relevance assessments: 137 direct, 307 context and 196 not established. It also contains 130 selected risk records and 56 incident cases. These are not eight completed studies or 80 incidents. The November 2 mapping and challenge were AI-assisted editorial work, not independent human or legal validation or a new full-text review. Counts describe this selected collection, not population coverage. Associations do not establish causation, compliance or control effectiveness.

The website JSON records research-crosswalk.json, risk-selection.json, incidents.json and map-clusters.json under /assets/governance/ are candidate inputs for a future versioned inventory. Import or reuse requires source-level provenance and rights checks; the explorer is not a benchmark answer key. The separately shared project conversation was not retrieved for this charter.

A future cross-study catalog should distinguish study, paper, incident report, risk statement, control, legal provision and evaluation artifact. Each record needs a stable identifier, exact source locator, date/version, unit of analysis, access and reuse conditions, source status, and the claim it supports. Keep allegations, verified events, hypotheses and executed results distinct.

Record a proposed connection as a testable relation: which records connect, the proposed mechanism, direct source support, competing explanations, duplication or common-source dependence, uncertainty, and what evidence would reject it. Similar terminology or repeated reports are not independent confirmation. Risk taxonomies and listed mitigations can inform cases, but do not establish severity, effectiveness or prevalence.

Use governance maps for discovery. Verify material legal claims against applicable primary provisions, recording jurisdiction, actor, use case, legal status and dates. A crosswalk or test result does not establish compliance or certification. Public access does not establish an open reuse license.

## Authority, safety and disclosure

Work within each assignment's actual approval. This document grants no standing permission to publish, spend, recruit participants, contact third parties, transmit private data, create credentials, expand access or run experiments. Repositories remain private unless separately authorized. Keep client information, unrelated personal records and internal assistant instructions out of research artifacts.

Preserve source snapshots, failures, corrections and decision rationale. Report what was proposed, implemented, executed, checked by AI, and checked by a human separately. A hash verifies byte consistency, not truth, authenticity or scientific validity.

Use the [workflow](WORKFLOW.md) for each assignment. Review this charter when responsibilities, study scope or permissions materially change; do not silently treat a proposed future arrangement as active.
